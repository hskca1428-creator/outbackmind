[Title] Dehydration prevention
In remote Australian conditions, dehydration can occur quickly. Drink water regularly, even if not thirsty. Urine colour should be pale. Avoid excessive caffeine and alcohol, and rest in shade during peak heat.

[Title] Heatstroke emergency
Heatstroke is life-threatening. Symptoms include confusion, no sweating, high body temperature, and collapse. Call emergency services immediately, cool the body rapidly with water, and move the person to shade.

[Title] Snake bite emergency
Apply pressure immobilisation bandage, keep the patient still, and call emergency services immediately. Do not wash the bite area.

[Title] Spider bite precautions
Most spider bites are not life-threatening, but severe symptoms like pain, sweating, nausea, or muscle cramps require urgent medical attention.

[Title] Bushfire survival
If caught near a bushfire, seek shelter in a cleared area or solid structure. Cover exposed skin, stay low, and monitor emergency broadcasts.

[Title] Flash flood danger
Floodwaters can rise quickly and are unpredictable. Never drive through water unless confirmed safe. Even shallow water can sweep vehicles away.

[Title] First aid kit essentials
A remote travel first aid kit should include bandages, antiseptic, compression bandage, pain relief, thermal blanket, and emergency contact information.

[Title] Insect protection
Use insect repellent, wear long sleeves, and avoid stagnant water areas to reduce bites from mosquitoes and flies.
